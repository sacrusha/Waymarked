The stately **black walnut** comes from North America. It is related to our common walnut. The tree can grow up to 45 metres tall. Its pinnate leaves grow up to 50 centimetres long. The yellow-green fruits resemble lemons. Inside is a very hard, edible nut.

The **ginkgo** by the park entrance next to the Kurtheater comes from the opposite end of the world. Ginkgos were cultivated as temple trees in China and Japan. In Europe they have been planted as ornamental trees since about 1730.

The broad, fan-shaped leaves are a peculiarity in the plant world. Already in the age of the dinosaurs, some 180 million years ago, ginkgos and related species existed almost all over the world. Today’s ginkgo is the last surviving representative of its plant group. That is why it is regarded as a “living fossil”.
