Natural stone walls are extreme habitats. There is hardly any water, little humus and large temperature swings. Some plants, however, have adapted well to these conditions.

The delicate **ivy-leaved toadflax** originally comes from Italy. It has developed a special method of spreading its seeds: after flowering, the ripe seed capsules turn towards the wall. That way the seeds do not fall into empty space but straight into the crevices in the wall.

The widespread **wall-rue** is an evergreen fern. As one of few higher plants, this species can dry out completely for a time and later carry on growing, much like mosses or algae.
