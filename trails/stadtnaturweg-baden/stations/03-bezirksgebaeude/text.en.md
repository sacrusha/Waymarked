The **Common Swift** is easily recognised by its shrill “sree-sree” calls. In the local dialect it is therefore also called “Spyr” or “Spyre”. From May to July, 20 to 30 pairs breed at the Bezirksgebäude. As soon as the young can fly, they set off on the long journey to their winter quarters in southern Africa.

Common Swifts breed in colonies. Each pair often uses the same entrance hole to its nest for many years. The small openings can be seen on the underside of the building’s roof.

Common Swifts are true aerial acrobats and reach speeds of over 100 kilometres per hour. They hunt flying insects. Outside the breeding season they spend day and night in the air and even sleep on the wing.
