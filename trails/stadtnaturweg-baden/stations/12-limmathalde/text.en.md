For eleven months of the year this place looks like an ordinary, shady deciduous forest. But in mid-March the picture changes: before the trees and shrubs come into leaf, a lush carpet of flowers in white and lilac glows here.

With the first warming rays of sunshine, the **hollow-root corydalis** (*Corydalis cava*), for example, shoots up. It manages its early start thanks to a tuber in the ground, in which it stores nutrients and water.

The corydalis slumbers in the ground for most of the year. The **spring snowflake** and the **wild garlic**, which also thrive here, do much the same.
