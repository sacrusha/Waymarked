The **sweet chestnut** is known for its edible fruit: the marroni. The Romans already brought the tree with them from Asia Minor. South of the Alps it found a new home. In Ticino especially, the species played an important role for centuries as a source of food and firewood.

North of the Alps the tree thrives only in particularly mild locations. These include wine-growing areas or warm south-facing slopes such as this one.

The **horse chestnut**, more common here, is not related to the sweet chestnut. The leaves of the sweet chestnut hang singly from the twig. The horse chestnut, by contrast, has palmate leaves, usually with five to seven leaflets. Its fruits are inedible for humans. Large horse chestnuts stand further down in the “Im Graben” green space.
